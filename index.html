Deep Dive · Data Science Flagship Project, 2026

Osteoarthritis Market Share Forecasting Pipeline

In plain English: a drug company's sales were growing, but the market data said doctors were prescribing its osteoarthritis injection far less. I tested whether anyone can forecast that trend, and found out first that the data itself could not be fully trusted.

Problem

A brand manager selling an osteoarthritis injection (Zilretta) needs an early signal when competing steroid and NSAID treatments take doctor-visit share. The one market dataset (IQVIA visit counts) disagreed with the company's own sales.

Why

A sales target or forecast built on a number that may be wrong is worse than no forecast. Before predicting anything, the work had to show whether the data and the models could be trusted.

How

A Bronze/Silver/Gold SQLite warehouse over 72 months of IQVIA visits and 171 public CMS, CDC, SEC and FDA files, 7 leakage-tested feature groups, then models held to simple rules in walk-forward testing (about 46 test months). The pass/fail rules were written before any model ran.

What the data showed, without the jargon

1. Sales grew while recorded doctor visits fell

Both cannot be the whole truth, so the size of the visit decline should not be quoted as a market fact.

2. The drop is concentrated in two groups

Tells a field or marketing team where to look first.

3. Input groups that earned their place

Price, company sales and promotion did not predict monthly share, so they should not drive a monthly forecast.

Can a model beat the simple rule "same as last month"?

  • Forecasting the national share No 0 of 3 models won at 1, 3 or 6 months. The simple rule was kept.
  • Forecasting each segment's share A little Better in all 46 test months at 1 month (+0.44%), but forecasts ran 5.5% too high.
  • Alerting on a segment about to lose 30% of share Weak A 1-month supporting flag only. Much of it is regression to the mean.

Business impact: four decisions this analysis supports

1
Do not quote the visit drop yet

Sales rose 5% and 6% while visits fell 14% and 36%. A target built on that decline could be wrong by a large factor until IQVIA confirms panel coverage.

2
Review orthopedic surgery and physician assistants first

They are 64% of the net fall of 11,256 visits, so that is where a field or marketing review pays off soonest.

3
Shortlist five states for growth

Michigan, Arizona, Florida, Minnesota and Indiana have the most room to grow, on a ranking that held steady between 2022 and 2024 (0.92).

4
Skip the costlier national model

Last month's value beat all three trained models, so plan on it with its range. A model would add cost and no accuracy.

Status: analysis and modelling are complete. The stakeholder website and question box are planned, and nothing is deployed. These are decisions the analysis supports, not measured revenue impact.

This is where principle 02 (baseline before complexity) isn't an abstraction: every model had to beat "same as last month" under rules written before it ran. For the national number none did, and reporting that plainly is the result.

Deep Dive · Penn State Research Assistant, 2026

Resume–JD Matching via Deep Learning

Problem

Screening RA candidates by keyword matching misses people who describe their skills differently than a project listing does, and over-ranks resumes that just repeat the right buzzwords.

Why

A ranking system that can't tell semantic fit from keyword-stuffing produces bad shortlists on both ends: real candidates filtered out, weak ones let through.

How

A 3-stage pipeline that only adds complexity when it earns its place: a TF-IDF baseline first, then SBERT semantic embeddings, then a supervised neural layer that outputs a calibrated match probability instead of a raw similarity score, reaching 96% accuracy across 120 resumes and 2,277 projects.

384-ddense embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2)

This is where principles 02 (baseline before complexity) and 03 (AI accelerates the analysis, it doesn't replace the validation) aren't abstractions: TF-IDF proved the floor, SBERT proved the ceiling was higher, and every embedding-based match still had to earn its place against a supervised, labeled baseline before it became a trusted probability.
